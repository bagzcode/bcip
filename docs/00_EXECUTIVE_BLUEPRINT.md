# 00 — Executive Blueprint

## 1. Vision

Build a shared digital ecosystem where batik heritage knowledge, measurable color science, explainable AI, and fashion-design exploration operate on the same governed dataset.

The platform should serve four purposes simultaneously:

1. **Heritage preservation:** document motifs, colors, processes, narratives, and contributor knowledge with provenance.
2. **Design support:** help artisans and designers explore palettes and garment applications without replacing their authorship.
3. **Research infrastructure:** produce analysis-ready quantitative datasets and reproducible experiments.
4. **Education and public engagement:** make Batik Lasem knowledge understandable through visual exploration and source-grounded conversation.

## 2. Pilot scope

The initial domain is **Batik Lasem**, beginning with the existing 102 Isen-Isen collection and additional physical batik samples gathered under approved consent and governance arrangements.

The platform must allow later extension to other batik traditions without assuming that Lasem concepts, meanings, or palette rules are universal.

## 3. Product ecosystem

### Motif Explorer

The central catalogue and discovery interface. Users can browse and search motifs, samples, palettes, artisans, techniques, narratives, and sources. It is also the single source of truth for identifiers and metadata used by all other modules.

### Hue Seer

The color-science module. It analyzes images, extracts palettes, calculates standardized features, compares samples, supports controlled recoloring, and later recommends culturally appropriate palettes.

### Lasem Guru

The knowledge and explainable-AI module. It answers questions using approved source material and expert annotations, always showing citations, provenance, confidence, and disagreement where applicable.

### Dress Weaver

The fashion-application module. It lets users place motifs on 2D garment templates, change scale and orientation, map approved palettes, save versions, and collect expert or consumer evaluations.

### Research Lab

The publication-data module. Researchers define controlled stimuli, participant groups, survey instruments, tasks, conditions, consent, and exports. Every experiment records software version, dataset version, model version, and randomization seed.

### Governance Console

The cultural and data-rights module. Data stewards manage contributor consent, attribution, licensing, permitted uses, access tiers, review status, withdrawal, and audit history.

## 4. User groups and roles

- **Public visitor:** browse public content and educational material.
- **Registered learner:** save collections and learning progress.
- **Designer:** create garment and palette projects.
- **Artisan/contributor:** submit or annotate knowledge and review representation of their work.
- **Cultural expert:** validate claims, annotations, and recommendation constraints.
- **Researcher:** create governed datasets and experiments.
- **Data steward:** manage consent, licensing, access tiers, and releases.
- **Administrator:** manage platform operations; does not automatically have permission to disclose culturally restricted content.

Use permissions, not only role names. A user may hold several roles within an organization or project.

## 5. Core research questions supported

- Which measurable color properties distinguish batik collections, production methods, periods, and intended uses?
- How do color properties influence authenticity, cultural identity, emotion, aesthetics, memorability, premium perception, and purchase intention?
- Can a recommendation model produce attractive palettes while obeying expert-defined cultural constraints?
- How do explanations, confidence, and editable recommendations affect trust, perceived control, and creativity?
- Which findings generalize, and which remain specific to a region, community, or context?

## 6. Success definition by stage

### Year 1

- Trusted community and ethics framework.
- Reproducible capture and color-analysis workflow.
- Governed Batik Lasem dataset.
- Motif Explorer and Hue Seer MVP.
- Limited source-grounded Lasem Guru.
- Simple Dress Weaver preview.
- Research export and pilot quantitative study.

### Year 2

- Controlled color-perception studies.
- Cultural knowledge base and explainable palette recommendation.
- Designer workflow evaluation.
- More complete Dress Weaver and Research Lab.

### Year 3

- Longitudinal artisan/SME deployment.
- Governed dataset releases and selected cross-region validation.
- Technology-transfer package, training, software registration, and maintenance model.

## 7. Explicit non-goals for the first release

- Photorealistic virtual try-on.
- Fully autonomous batik generation.
- Universal claims about the meaning of colors.
- Public release of all raw images and cultural knowledge.
- Separate microservice for every module.
- Training a large model from scratch.
- Replacing artisans, cultural experts, or curators.
